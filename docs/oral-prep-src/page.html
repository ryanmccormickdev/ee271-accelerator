Set up a mock exam

One of you examines and one answers. The examiner reads each question aloud and sees the model answer, the Our design prompt and suggested follow-ups. The exam, as far as we know

  • A 15-minute oral quiz on the technical details of the project, Thu Dec 3, worth 25% of the course grade.
  • Staff: "make sure you can explain any AI-assisted work in your own words." Whether partners are examined separately or together hasn't been announced. Online students take it over Zoom.
  • With follow-ups, roughly 6 to 8 questions fit in 15 minutes, so plan on about two minutes per topic. The examiner will interrupt and redirect; that's normal, not a bad sign.

The pair protocol

  1. Pick roles. The examiner takes the screen (Mock exam, Examiner view). The examinee closes the code, the notes and this page.
  2. Read the question aloud, as written, and start the clock.
  3. Answer out loud. The answer in one sentence first, then how it works, then one concrete example (a number, a line of code, a clock cycle), then how you know it's right. Then stop and let the examiner ask.
  4. Push. The examiner asks at least one follow-up per question: "Why that way?", "What would happen if…?", "Where is that in your code?", "How did you check it?". Suggested follow-ups are under each model answer.
  5. Grade honestly. Got it: correct, complete, no help needed. Shaky: the right idea but a missing piece, or it needed a hint. Missed: wrong or stuck. Grades go into the examinee's progress, so missed and shaky questions come back sooner in Drill.
  6. Swap roles and run a second mock with a fresh plan.
  7. Rotate topics. Each of you defends the parts you did not write. If you wrote the PE, take the buffer and controller questions (Drill, Tag filter); the oral exam can ask either of you about anything.
  8. The same day, drill the review list from the summary (Drill, Show: the review list).

No notes. You won't have any in the exam, so practise the same way. When you don't know, say what you do know and how you would find out. Don't bluff: the follow-up question will find it.

Drilling alone

  • Ten minutes a day beats an hour once. Say each answer out loud before you press Reveal, then grade yourself against the model answer.
  • Spaced repetition: a missed question comes back after 2 others, a shaky one after 5, and a known one after 12, then 25, 50 and 100 graded questions. Unseen questions are mixed in, core ones first.
  • Aim with the filters: one bank, one tag (for example rnd or timing), or only missed or shaky.
  • Each person has their own progress. Switch with the names at the top; rename them with Rename. Progress is saved in this browser only.

Filling in the Our design fields

Many questions end with a yellow Fill in from your design prompt. The model answer covers the general case, but the examiner will ask about your design: your cycle, your file and line, your numbers. Write your answer into the bank file, on that question's Our design line, after the word Ours:. Before:

- **Our design:** Which PE count did you choose, and what stopped you going higher?

After:

- **Our design:** Which PE count did you choose, and what stopped you going higher? Ours: <what you chose>, because <the number that decided it> (<the report or file it comes from>).

Then rebuild the page: python3 docs/oral-prep-src/build.py. Your answer appears in green under the prompt, Browse counts how many prompts each bank has filled in, and each yellow box names the file to edit and the line where its question starts. Commit the .md file so your partner gets it too. Update them:

  • after each module passes its testbench: what it does, in which cycle, in which file and line;
  • after each synthesis run: area, worst slack, the clock period;
  • after each Part 3 experiment: the FoM before and after, and why it moved.

The numbers will change as the design changes. Keep only the latest, and say which version they come from.

Adding questions

After you finish a module, add the questions you'd ask your partner about it: the ones that made you think. A pull request's "Explain it" box (the teach-back) is already an oral answer; turn it into a question. Each bank is a Markdown file in docs/oral-prep/; the format and the rules are in docs/oral-prep/README.md. Rebuild with python3 docs/oral-prep-src/build.py; it prints a warning for every entry it had to skip, and saved progress survives as long as a question's wording doesn't change.

Words this page uses

Bank
One Markdown file of questions in docs/oral-prep/, such as part1.md.
Core, deeper, stretch
Must know cold; a likely follow-up; a hard follow-up.
Our design
A prompt for the part of an answer that only your design can supply.
Spaced repetition
Showing a question again after a gap that grows each time you get it right and shrinks when you miss it.
Due
A question whose gap has run out. Drill shows due questions first.
Examiner, examinee
The one asking, who sees the answers, and the one answering, who sees only the question.
Follow-up
A deeper question on the same tags, asked straight after a core one.
Plan code
A number that recreates the same mock exam on a second screen.
